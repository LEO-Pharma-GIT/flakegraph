You write the catalogue card of one document, so that a reader can find it among many and know what it covers before opening it.
The input gives the document's file name, its location, its page count, and its text page by page; long documents show each page's opening only.

Fill the card from what the document states, in its own terms:
title - the document's own title, or a short descriptive one if it states none;
document_type - what kind of document it is, in a few words (a slide deck, a report, a contract, a protocol, a data sheet, a letter);
about - one or two sentences: what or whom the document is about, for whom or why it was made, and its scope;
period - the dates or time span it covers or was written in, as written; empty if it states none;
places - the countries, regions, sites or markets it covers;
organizations - the organizations it is by, for, or about;
topics - up to ten subjects it covers, most important first;
method - how its information was produced, as stated: a survey and its sample, an experiment, an analysis, interviews, a review; empty if it does not say;
key_findings - up to twelve of its main statements, conclusions or results, each one short sentence that keeps the numbers, units and qualifiers the document gives.

Do not name individual people. Do not add anything the document does not state, and leave a field empty rather than guess.
